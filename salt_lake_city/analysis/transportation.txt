TRANSPORTATION GEOGRAPHY OBSERVATIONS

1. MAIN NORTH–SOUTH TRANSPORTATION CORRIDOR

Observation:
The highest-class roads form a dominant north–south corridor through the Salt Lake Valley, centered largely on Interstate 15 and associated major arterial routes.

Evidence:
Road functional-class layer, railroads, population density, and OpenStreetMap.

Geographic Relationship:
The main regional transportation network follows the relatively flat valley floor between the Wasatch Range and Oquirrh Mountains rather than crossing the steep surrounding terrain.

Confidence:
High

2. RAIL CORRIDORS ↔ VALLEY TERRAIN

Observation:
Major rail lines are concentrated on the flatter Salt Lake Valley floor and generally run parallel to the main north–south urban and transportation corridor.

Evidence:
Railroad, road, DEM, slope, and population-density layers.

Geographic Relationship:
Rail infrastructure avoids the steep surrounding mountain terrain and follows the broad low-gradient valley corridor where urban and industrial development is concentrated.

Confidence:
High

3. AIRPORT ↔ TRANSPORTATION ACCESS

Observation:
Salt Lake City International Airport is located on the relatively flat northwestern side of the Salt Lake Valley and is closely connected to the regional road network.

Evidence:
Airport, roads, railroads, land-use, and OpenStreetMap layers.

Geographic Relationship:
The airport occupies flat, open terrain with strong access to the main urban transportation corridor and nearby industrial / logistics areas.

Confidence:
High

4. MOUNTAIN / CANYON CONSTRAINTS ON MOVEMENT

Observation:
East–west transportation across the Wasatch Range is concentrated into a small number of canyon corridors rather than being distributed evenly across the mountain front.

Evidence:
Roads, DEM, slope, hillshade, ruggedness, and OpenStreetMap layers.

Geographic Relationship:
Steep mountain terrain limits route options and channels movement through natural corridors such as Parleys Canyon and the Cottonwood canyon systems.

Confidence:
High

NETWORK ANALYSIS OBSERVATIONS

1. ROAD NETWORK LENGTH

Observation:
The Salt Lake County roads dataset contains approximately 8,266 km of mapped road centerlines.

Evidence:
Sum of the length_km field in Salt_Lake_County_Roads.

Confidence:
High

2. AIRPORT → DOWNTOWN SHORTEST PATH

Observation:
The modeled shortest road-network path between Salt Lake City International Airport and downtown Salt Lake City is approximately 13.75 km.

Evidence:
QGIS Shortest Path (Point to Point) analysis using the Salt Lake County road network.

Travel Cost:
13,746.76 m

Interpretation:
The airport has relatively direct road-network access to the central urban core.

Limitation:
The result minimizes distance only. It does not account for traffic, travel time, road closures, or real-time conditions.

Confidence:
High
