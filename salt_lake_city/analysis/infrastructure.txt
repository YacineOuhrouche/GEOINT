INFRASTRUCTURE GEOGRAPHY OBSERVATIONS

1. WATER SERVICE COVERAGE ↔ URBAN DEVELOPMENT

Observation:
Public culinary water service areas are concentrated across the developed Salt Lake Valley and generally 
correspond with the main urbanized and populated areas.

Evidence:
Water-service, population-density, land-use, municipal-boundary, and road layers.

Geographic Relationship:
Water-service infrastructure follows the main settlement pattern of the valley and becomes less extensive toward steep mountain terrain and sparsely developed peripheral areas.

Confidence:
High

2. INDUSTRIAL / LOGISTICS CLUSTERING

Observation:
Industrial and logistics land uses are concentrated mainly in the flatter western and northwestern parts of the Salt Lake Valley, 
especially near major highways, rail corridors, and Salt Lake City International Airport.

Evidence:
Land-use, roads, railroads, airport, and OpenStreetMap layers.

Geographic Relationship:
Industrial activity clusters where flat terrain, large parcels, and multimodal transportation access coincide.

Confidence:
High

3. INTERMODAL CONNECTIVITY

Observation:
The strongest infrastructure concentration occurs where major highways, rail corridors, airport access, and industrial land uses overlap in the Salt Lake Valley.

Evidence:
Roads, railroads, airport, land-use, and OpenStreetMap layers.

Geographic Relationship:
Multimodal access strengthens the western and northwestern Salt Lake Valley as a major logistics and infrastructure zone.

Confidence:
High
