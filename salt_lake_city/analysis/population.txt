POPULATION & SETTLEMENT OBSERVATIONS

1. POPULATION CONCENTRATION

Observation:
Population density is concentrated primarily on the Salt Lake Valley floor, especially across the continuous urban corridor around Salt Lake City and its surrounding municipalities.

Evidence:
2020 census block-group population density layer.

Geographic Relationship:
Population density decreases sharply toward the Wasatch Range, Oquirrh Mountains, and other rugged peripheral terrain.

Confidence:
High

2. POPULATION ↔ TERRAIN

Observation:
The highest population densities are concentrated on the relatively flat Salt Lake Valley floor, while population density decreases substantially on the steeper slopes of the Wasatch Range and Oquirrh Mountains.

Evidence:
Population-density layer combined with slope and DEM layers.

Geographic Relationship:
Flatter terrain supports denser urban development, while steep and rugged terrain strongly limits settlement.

Confidence:
High

3. POPULATION ↔ TRANSPORTATION

Observation:
Higher population densities are concentrated along the main transportation corridor running through the Salt Lake Valley, while lower-density areas are more common toward the mountainous edges of the county.

Evidence:
Population-density, roads, and railroad layers.

Geographic Relationship:
Transportation infrastructure is concentrated in the same relatively flat valley corridor where most population and urban development are located.

Confidence:
High


4. POPULATION ↔ WATER

Observation:
The Jordan River passes through the developed Salt Lake Valley, while population density generally decreases toward the Great Salt Lake shoreline and the steeper mountain drainage areas.

Evidence:
Population-density, major-streams, lakes, DEM, and OpenStreetMap layers.

Geographic Relationship:
Water features intersect the urban valley, but the most densely populated areas are concentrated on the broader buildable valley floor rather than directly along lake margins or steep drainage terrain.

Confidence:
Moderate to High
