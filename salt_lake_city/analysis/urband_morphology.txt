URBAN MORPHOLOGY & LAND-USE OBSERVATIONS

1. URBAN DEVELOPMENT PATTERN

Observation:
Urban land use is concentrated across the Salt Lake Valley, forming a broad continuous developed corridor around Salt Lake City and surrounding municipalities.

Evidence:
Land-use, municipal-boundary, road, and OpenStreetMap layers.

Geographic Relationship:
Development is concentrated on the flatter valley floor, while mountainous terrain and large open areas form clear limits to the urbanized region.

Confidence:
Highs

2. URBAN EDGE ↔ TERRAIN CONSTRAINTS

Observation:
The urbanized area expands broadly across the Salt Lake Valley but becomes much less continuous near the Wasatch Range and Oquirrh Mountains.

Evidence:
Land-use, slope, hillshade, DEM, and municipal-boundary layers.

Geographic Relationship:
Steeper and more rugged terrain creates a strong physical limit to outward urban expansion, producing a clearer urban edge along the mountain fronts.

Confidence:
High

3. URBAN FORM ↔ TRANSPORTATION

Observation:
Urban development is strongly aligned with the main north–south transportation corridor through the Salt Lake Valley, where major roads and rail lines pass through the most continuously developed areas.

Evidence:
Land-use, roads, railroads, population-density, and municipal-boundary layers.

Geographic Relationship:
Transportation infrastructure and urban development are concentrated in the same broad valley corridor, while steep mountain terrain limits alternative large-scale development patterns.

Confidence:
High

4. INDUSTRIAL / LOGISTICS LAND USE

Observation:
Industrial and logistics land uses are concentrated mainly in the flatter western and northwestern parts of the Salt Lake Valley, especially near major road, rail, and airport infrastructure.

Evidence:
Land-use, roads, railroads, airport, and OpenStreetMap layers.

Geographic Relationship:
Industrial activity tends to cluster where large parcels, flat terrain, and strong transportation access coincide.

Confidence:
Moderate to High
