CHOKEPOINTS, CORRIDORS & BARRIER CROSSINGS

1. I-15 / CENTRAL SALT LAKE VALLEY CORRIDOR

Observation:
Interstate 15 forms the dominant north–south transportation corridor through Salt Lake County and 
passes through the central urbanized valley.

Geographic Context:
The corridor occupies relatively flat terrain between the Wasatch Range and Oquirrh Mountains.

Why Movement Concentrates Here:
The broad valley floor provides the easiest continuous north–south route through the county while the surrounding mountain ranges strongly constrain alternative regional corridors.

Alternatives:
Parallel arterial roads and local road networks provide some redundancy, but major regional movement remains concentrated in the central valley corridor.

Evidence:
Road hierarchy, DEM, slope, population density, land use, and OpenStreetMap.

Confidence:
High

2. PARLEYS CANYON / I-80 CORRIDOR

Observation:
Interstate 80 leaves the Salt Lake Valley through Parleys Canyon, creating one of the main east–west transportation corridors through the Wasatch Range.

Geographic Context:
The canyon cuts through steep and rugged mountain terrain east of Salt Lake City.

Why Movement Concentrates Here:
The canyon provides a comparatively accessible natural passage through terrain that is otherwise difficult to cross.

Alternatives:
Other mountain routes exist, but they are fewer, less direct, or more constrained than the Parleys Canyon corridor.

Evidence:
Roads, DEM, slope, hillshade, ruggedness, and OpenStreetMap.

Confidence:
High

3. POINT OF THE MOUNTAIN / SOUTHERN VALLEY GATEWAY

Observation:
The southern end of the Salt Lake Valley narrows around the Point of the Mountain area, where major transportation routes pass between surrounding elevated terrain.

Geographic Context:
The corridor lies between the southern Wasatch foothills and nearby upland terrain, creating a narrower passage than the central valley.

Why Movement Concentrates Here:
North–south regional movement is funneled through this relatively narrow low-gradient corridor, including Interstate 15.

Alternatives:
Local roads provide some redundancy, but large-scale regional movement remains concentrated in the same general gateway.

Evidence:
Roads, DEM, slope, hillshade, population density, and OpenStreetMap.

Confidence:
High

4. AIRPORT / WESTERN LOGISTICS GATEWAY

Observation:
Salt Lake City International Airport and nearby industrial/logistics areas form a major transportation and infrastructure concentration on the western side of the Salt Lake Valley.

Geographic Context:
The area occupies relatively flat terrain with direct access to major highways and rail infrastructure.

Why Movement Concentrates Here:
Airport access, highway connectivity, rail access, and available flat land combine to concentrate logistics and freight-related movement in this part of the valley.

Alternatives:
Other industrial areas exist elsewhere in the county, but few combine airport, highway, rail, and large flat parcels as strongly.

Evidence:
Airport, roads, railroads, land use, DEM, and OpenStreetMap.

Confidence:
High
